# store consciousness
> 校准自「storehouse-consciousness」，依 Cook, *Three Texts on Consciousness Only*。

**梵 / Sanskrit**：*ālaya-vijñāna*

## Explanation（English）
A transliteration of the Sanskrit ālaya. The Consciousness-Only school posits eight consciousnesses—eye, ear, nose, tongue, body, mental, manas, and ālaya—and this is one of them. Also written ālīya-consciousness or ālaya-consciousness; abbreviated “laya.” The old translation renders it “non-perishing consciousness,” the new translation “store consciousness.” It is also called the eighth consciousness, the root consciousness, the abode consciousness, or the non-perishing consciousness. Its meaning is that it grasps and maintains the seeds of all dharmas without losing or spoiling them. This consciousness is the root of all existence in the universe and contains all existence, hence “store consciousness”; and because it can store the seeds that give rise to the myriad dharmas, it is also called the “seed consciousness.” Among the “three transformers” it is called the “first transforming consciousness.” The Extensive Mahāyāna Treatise on the Five Aggregates says: “The ālaya-consciousness can gather and store all seeds; it also gathers and stores the mark of self-pride; and it takes the body as its cognitive object. It is also called the ādāna-consciousness because it grasps and maintains the body.” As the treatise states, the ālaya gathers and stores all seeds and gives rise to the myriad dharmas, hence it is called the source of the universe and of life. “Ālaya” is rendered by meaning as “store consciousness.” “Store” has three senses: the storing (active), the stored, and the grasped-as-store. As the “storing”: this consciousness stores the seeds of the myriad dharmas—the consciousness being that which stores, the seeds being what is stored. As the “stored”: the seeds give rise to activity and are perfumed by the seven evolving consciousnesses, and the newly perfumed seeds are stored back into this consciousness—here the new seeds are that which stores and this consciousness is what is stored. As the “grasped-as-store”: the seventh consciousness mistakenly grasps the perceiving-part of the eighth as a “self”; from the standpoint of the seventh's grasping of this consciousness, it is called the “grasped-as-store,” also the “self-love grasping-store.” The ālaya grasps and maintains the seeds of the myriad dharmas and gives rise to them—this is called “ālaya-based dependent arising.” In fact, what is called “consciousness” is only a function. In its latent state this function is not called consciousness but “seed”; when the seed becomes active and gives rise to “activity,” it is called not seed but consciousness. When seeds give rise to activity and produce the very substance of the ālaya-consciousness, this is called in the transformation of consciousness the “causal transformer”; simultaneously with the causal transformer, the ālaya gives rise to the first seven consciousnesses, and upon each consciousness-substance there arise the two parts of image and perceiving—this is called the “resultant transformer.” The image-part is the differentiated appearances of the myriad dharmas of the universe; the perceiving-part is the subjective act of cognition. Only when the perceiving-part (subjective cognition) cognizes the image-part (the appearances of the objective myriad dharmas) is there what we call the universe and life. This function—whereby the seeds of the myriad dharmas within the ālaya, relying on condition as cause, give rise to the phenomenal world—is precisely ālaya-based dependent arising.
