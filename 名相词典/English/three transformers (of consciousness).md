# three transformers (of consciousness)

## Explanation（English）
The Consciousness-Only school holds that “consciousness” can transform and give rise to all dharmas, or can manifest as the subject of the subjective (seeing part) and objective (seen part) cognitive functions within the mind—hence called “transformer.” According to the character of its function, there are three transformers: retribution, deliberation, and cognizing objects. (1) The retribution transformer, also called the first or initial transformer, is the eighth, ālaya (store consciousness). The eighth is the retribution each of the beings of the six destinies possesses; drawn by projecting karma, it is the continuously abiding total-retribution body—hence called retribution, or retribution transformer, consciousness as retribution, or true retribution. The first six consciousnesses (eye, ear, nose, tongue, body, mental) and individual retributions such as poverty or wealth, nobility or baseness, are particular-retribution bodies arising from completing karma; yet because that retribution-body can be interrupted, they are not called retribution but “born of retribution.” From the standpoint of the three stages of the ālaya, the name “consciousness as retribution” corresponds to the stage of the fruition of good and evil karma. (2) The deliberating transformer, also called the second transformer, is the seventh, manas. Because the seventh constantly and carefully deliberates, it is called the deliberating transformer. Although both the sixth and the seventh can deliberate, the sixth has interruptions and so is not called the deliberating transformer. (3) The object-cognizing transformer, also called the third transformer, in full “transformer that discerns objects,” refers to the first six consciousnesses (eye, ear, nose, tongue, body, mental). These six clearly discern salient objects (such as form, sound, and so on), hence “transformer that discerns objects.” See the Thirty Verses on Consciousness-Only.
