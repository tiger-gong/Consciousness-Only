# Thirty Verses on Consciousness-Only
**梵 / Sanskrit**：*Triṃśikā*

## Explanation（English）
In one fascicle. Composed by the bodhisattva Vasubandhu, translated by the Tang Tripiṭaka-master Xuanzang. Also called the Treatise in Thirty Verses on Consciousness-Only, the Verses of the Thirty-Verse Treatise, the Thirty-Verse Treatise on Consciousness-Only, and so on. Among the “one root and ten branches” it is called the “branch that raises high the Dharma-banner.” In his late years Vasubandhu composed the thirty verses, but passed away before composing a prose commentary; thereafter the ten great commentator-masters—Bandhuśrī, Citrabhāna, and others—each composed a commentary on the thirty verses. When Xuanzang traveled to study in India, he brought back the commentaries of the ten houses, and blending their works he translated them as the Cheng weishi lun (Treatise on the Establishment of Consciousness-Only).
