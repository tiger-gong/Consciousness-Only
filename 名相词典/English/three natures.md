# three natures
**梵 / Sanskrit**：*tri-svabhāva*

## Explanation（English）
Also called the three self-natures: the imagined nature, the other-dependent nature, and the perfectly accomplished nature. These three self-natures are the root of all Dharma meanings in the Consciousness-Only school. The Dharma-characteristics school takes Consciousness-Only as the Middle Way—precisely revealing neither emptiness nor existence. The practitioner carefully contemplates all dharmas: none lean toward existence alone, none toward emptiness alone. Among the three self-natures, the other-dependent nature is the center. All dharmas arise depending on causes and conditions; if sentient beings, upon these provisional dharmas of causal combination, cling and calculate them as a real self or real dharmas, that is the imagined nature; if upon the provisional dharmas of the other-dependent they understand that all arise dependently and have no substantial self-nature, that is the perfectly accomplished nature. The imagined nature is delusive existence on the side of emotional cognition; the other-dependent nature is provisional existence of causal combination; the perfectly accomplished nature is the true suchness that is perfect, accomplished, and real. Cheng weishi lun, fascicle 8, says: mind and mental factors and what they manifest, arising from manifold conditions, are like illusory things and the like—all are named other-dependent nature; the foolish thereon falsely cling to self and dharmas—all are named imagined; upon the other-dependent, those falsely clung-to self and dharmas are both empty, and the true nature of consciousness and so on revealed by that emptiness is named perfectly accomplished.
