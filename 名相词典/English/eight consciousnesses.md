# eight consciousnesses

## Explanation（English）
The first category among the Five Categories and Hundred Dharmas established by the Consciousness-Only school: the eight ruling minds (citta-rāja), abbreviated as the “eight consciousnesses”—the eye, ear, nose, tongue, body, mental (mano-vijñāna), manas, and ālaya consciousnesses. The first six are named after the faculty each depends on; the manas is “mind” and is named after its own nature; the ālaya, in the sense of grasping and maintaining the causes and effects of all dharmas, is likewise named from its own nature. The first five are called the first five consciousnesses, the mental consciousness the sixth, the manas the seventh, and the ālaya the eighth. Since the consciousnesses from the eye up to the manas arise from the ālaya, they are collectively called the evolving consciousnesses (or seven evolving consciousnesses); the ālaya, being their cause, is called the root consciousness or seed consciousness. In the Thirty Verses the eight are divided into three transformers: the ālaya is the first transformer, the manas the second, the first six the third. As to nature: the first six take discernment as their nature, cognize the six objects (form, sound, smell, taste, touch, dharmas), and pertain to all three moral natures (good, evil, neutral); the manas takes constant deliberation as its nature, is of obscuring-neutral nature, and takes only the perceiving-portion of the ālaya as its inner self; the ālaya is of non-obscuring-neutral nature and, with a subtle mode of activity, cognizes the receptacle-world, the seeds, and the sentient body-with-faculties that it itself manifests. See the Baifa wenda chao.
