# ādāna-consciousness (appropriating consciousness)
**梵 / Sanskrit**：*ādāna-vijñāna*

## Explanation（English）
Ādāna is Sanskrit, rendered “appropriating consciousness”; there are two explanations. (1) An alternative name for the eighth consciousness. Because the ādāna-consciousness is the root consciousness that appropriates and upholds the sense faculties and the body so that they do not decay; further appropriates and upholds the seeds of all dharmas so that they are not lost; and again, by appropriating its own continuum, effects the continuity of rebirth—hence “appropriating consciousness.” New translators of the Dharma-characteristics school such as Xuanzang and Kuiji held that because it upholds the force of good and evil karma and the body of sentient beings so that they do not decay, ādāna is an alternative name for the eighth, ālaya (storehouse-consciousness). (2) Old translators left it unexplained and took it as an alternative name for the seventh consciousness. Because ādāna upholds seeds and the sentient body, while the manas-consciousness is constantly concomitant with the four afflictions of self-delusion, self-view, self-conceit, and self-love, and constantly and carefully takes the perceiving portion of the eighth, ālaya, as “I” and “mine”—the meanings the two represent are in fact the same; hence the old translators of the Dilun and Shelun schools took ādāna as an alternative name for the manas that appropriates the ālaya as a self. It is also one of the alternate names of the ālaya: rendered by meaning as “appropriating,” this consciousness can hold seeds and appropriate the faculty-body, hence ādāna-consciousness. See Saṃdhinirmocana-sūtra, fascicle 1.
