# causes and conditions
**梵 / Sanskrit**：*hetu-pratyaya*

## Explanation（English）
A joint term for cause (hetu) and condition (pratyaya), and one of the four conditions established by the Consciousness-Only school. “Cause” is the direct inner factor that gives rise to a result; “condition” refers to the indirect factor of external assistance. Accordingly, “causes and conditions” are also spoken of as inner cause and outer condition, or intimate cause and remote condition. Broadly, “cause” itself means cause-and-condition, including both inner cause and outer condition. All existents arise and cease through the gathering and dispersing of causes and conditions; this is called “arising from causes and conditions” or “dependent arising.” Hence all dharmas that arise and cease through causes and conditions are called “dharmas of conditioned arising and ceasing”; and the result produced by the union of cause and condition is called the “union of causes and conditions.” All existents provisionally arise through the union of causes and conditions and are without self-nature—this is the principle of “dependent arising, empty of nature.” Taking afflictions as cause and karma as condition, one calls forth the fruit of the deluded realm; taking wisdom as cause and concentration as condition, one calls forth the fruit of the awakened realm. “Cause-condition” (hetu-pratyaya) is also called the “intimate cause-condition,” the chief condition for the arising of any dharma—in fact, the seeds within the eighth, ālaya, consciousness that can give rise to present-active resultant dharmas. Fascicle 7 of the Cheng weishi lun says: “The cause-condition means that conditioned dharmas intimately produce their own fruit; this has two aspects—first, seeds; second, present activity.”
