# emptiness of self but reality of dharmas

## Explanation（English）
This means that the “self” is empty and void while “dharmas” are really existent. It is the position of the Hīnayāna Sarvāstivāda and similar schools. A sentient being's physical body is a provisional assemblage of the five aggregates and therefore has no permanent, unitary nature and no power of control—this is the “emptiness of self.” Yet regarding all other existent dharmas, they held that these are not empty and void but really possess an independent essence, hence “dharmas are existent.” Conversely, if all other existent dharmas are likewise regarded as empty, this is called the “twofold emptiness of self and dharmas”—the position of Mahāyāna Buddhism.
