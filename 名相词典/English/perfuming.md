# perfuming
**梵 / Sanskrit**：*vāsanā*

## Explanation（English）
The good and evil actions expressed through our body and speech, or the good and evil thoughts arising in our mind-consciousness, leave their “vapor-trace” within the ālaya-consciousness—like incense perfuming clothing; this is called perfuming (vāsanā). The actions produced through our body, speech, and mind are called “present activity” (現行). In other words, the eighth, ālaya, consciousness is able to preserve the traces of experience—these are the “vapor” or seeds. And the traces of experience (the deeds of body, speech, and mind) can influence a person's future character and conduct: this is perfuming. When one is habituated to doing good, that good conduct is a kind of perfuming; when one is habituated to doing evil, that evil conduct too is a kind of perfuming. Perfuming is not something deliberately contrived; it proceeds spontaneously, without one's being aware of it—like a person walking through mist, who does not intend to wet his clothes and does not feel them getting wet, yet in fact his clothes have become saturated with moisture. The Awakening of Mahāyāna Faith says: “As for the meaning of perfuming: worldly clothing has, in itself, no fragrance, but if a person perfumes it with incense, it then carries the scent.”
