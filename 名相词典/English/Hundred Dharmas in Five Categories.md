# Hundred Dharmas in Five Categories

## Explanation（English）
One of the classifications of dharmas. The Abhidharmakośa school posits Five Categories and Seventy-five Dharmas, whereas the Consciousness-Only school divides all dharmas into Five Categories and One Hundred Dharmas: (1) Mind-king dharmas, also called the eight ruling minds: the six consciousnesses (eye, ear, nose, tongue, body, mental), plus the seventh manas and the eighth ālaya—eight in all. (2) Mental-factor dharmas: fifty-one factors, in six divisions—universally active, object-specific, wholesome, afflictive, secondary afflictive, and indeterminate. (3) Form dharmas: the five faculties (eye, ear, nose, tongue, body), the five objects (form, sound, smell, taste, touch), and the “form included in the dharma-base” that is the object of mental consciousness—eleven in all. (4) Dharmas not concomitant with mind: twenty-four kinds such as attainment, life-faculty, generic likeness, the nature of dissimilar arising, the concentration of no-thought, the concentration of cessation, the retribution of no-thought, name-groups, phrase-groups, syllable-groups, arising, aging, abiding, impermanence, flowing-turning, definite distinction, correspondence, speed, sequence, place, time, number, the state of union, and the state of non-union. (5) Unconditioned dharmas: six kinds—space, cessation through discrimination, cessation not through discrimination, the cessation of the unmoving, the cessation of perception-and-feeling, and suchness. Of these five classes, all but the unconditioned dharmas belong to the conditioned. See the Treatise on the Hundred Dharmas.
