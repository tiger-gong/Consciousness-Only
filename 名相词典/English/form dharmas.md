# form dharmas
**梵 / Sanskrit**：*rūpa*

## Explanation（English）
The counterpart of “mind dharmas.” All dharmas are divided into the three schemata of aggregates, bases, and elements, taking the five aggregates as basic; the five aggregates, further condensed, are just the two dharmas of form and mind. “Form dharma,” broadly speaking, is the general term for material existence, referring to whatever has the nature of resistance—that is, whatever occupies a definite space, mutually obstructs self and other, and is liable to deterioration. If all dharmas are classified by the Five Categories and Hundred Dharmas, they divide into form dharmas, mind dharmas, mental-factor dharmas, dharmas not concomitant with mind, and unconditioned dharmas. Among these, form dharma is called the “form aggregate” in the five-aggregate classification, and mind dharma the “consciousness aggregate.” In the Consciousness-Only school's Five Categories and Hundred Dharmas, form dharmas are divided into three classes and eleven kinds: (1) the five faculties of eye, ear, nose, tongue, and body; (2) the five objects of form, sound, smell, taste, and touch; (3) unmanifest form. Within the Five Categories, form dharmas are placed after mind dharmas and mental-factor dharmas, because they cannot arise independently but are merely the manifestations of these former two.
