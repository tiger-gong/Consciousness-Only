# selflessness of dharmas
**梵 / Sanskrit**：*dharma-nairātmya*

## Explanation（English）
One of the two selflessnesses, the counterpart of “selflessness of persons.” It means that within all dharmas, conditioned and unconditioned, there is no substantial “self.” That is, all dharmas arise dependent on causes and conditions, are mutually dependent, and lack any substantial nature: conditioned dharmas have function yet do not abide permanently; unconditioned dharmas abide permanently yet have no function. “All dharmas are without self” is also one of the three Dharma-seals, called the seal “all dharmas are without self”; the three Dharma-seals are a crucial teaching of the Buddha-Dharma, and whether a teaching is ultimate is verified by these three. To understand that all dharmas arise from causes and conditions and truly have no self-nature or substance is the bodhisattva's path of contemplation, whereby one severs the hindrance to the knowable. The wisdom that thoroughly comprehends this principle is called the wisdom of the two selflessnesses.
