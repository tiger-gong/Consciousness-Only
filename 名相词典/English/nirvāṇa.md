# nirvāṇa
**梵 / Sanskrit**：*nirvāṇa*

## Explanation（English）
Also transliterated niyuan or nirvāṇa; rendered by meaning as extinction, quiescent extinction, extinction-crossing (deliverance), or no-arising. It is synonymous with such terms as “cessation through discrimination” (pratisaṃkhyā-nirodha), “freedom from bondage,” and “liberation.” It also appears as parinirvāṇa and mahāparinirvāṇa. Originally it meant “blowing out,” or the state of a fire gradually being extinguished; later it came to denote the state in which the fire of the afflictions is completely extinguished and the wisdom of awakening (i.e., bodhi) is perfected. This is the realm of awakening that transcends birth-and-death (the realm of delusion), and it is also the ultimate goal of Buddhist practice; therefore, as a mark of the Buddhist teaching, it is listed as one of the Dharma-seals, called “nirvāṇa is quiescence.” Sectarian (Abhidharma) Buddhism took nirvāṇa to be the state of having extinguished the afflictions. Within this there is a further division into nirvāṇa with remainder and nirvāṇa without remainder: the former is the case where the afflictions are severed yet the physical body (i.e., the remaining bodily support) still exists; the latter is the state of “body reduced to ashes and cognition extinguished,” that is, the situation in which everything returns to extinction. The physical death of the Buddha who appeared in this world as a human (specifically Śākyamuni) is called nirvāṇa, parinirvāṇa, or mahāparinirvāṇa. Entering nirvāṇa is also called “entering extinction” or “the fuel exhausted, the fire extinguished” (the fuel symbolizing the Buddha's body or the conditions for teaching, the fire symbolizing wisdom or the Buddha's body). Nirvāṇa originally referred to Śākyamuni's attainment of the Way, but nowadays it is generally used in the sense of nirvāṇa without remainder.
