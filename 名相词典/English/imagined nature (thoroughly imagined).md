# imagined nature (thoroughly imagined)
**梵 / Sanskrit**：*parikalpita-svabhāva*

## Explanation（English）
Also called the imagined characteristic; one of the three self-natures of Consciousness-Only. Ordinary beings’ deluded emotion thoroughly imagines and calculates the manifold dharmas arising from causes and conditions, clinging to them as really existent—this is the imagined nature. People generally take all worldly things as really existent, falsely clinging to the five aggregates, twelve bases, and eighteen elements as a real self and real dharmas, and thus thoroughly imagine; in truth all this is “only what consciousness transforms,” produced by sentient beings’ delusive discrimination. Nanda divides it into that which imagines and that which is imagined; Dharmapāla and Sthiramati divide it into that which imagines, that which is imagined, and the imagined [nature itself]—three doors. Generally, taking the sixth and seventh consciousnesses as the consciousnesses that imagine is the orthodox view.
