# six perfections
**梵 / Sanskrit**：*ṣaṭ-pāramitā*

## Explanation（English）
Translated as the “six perfections,” meaning “reaching the far shore”—that is, accomplishing and completing the ideal. These are the six virtues that a bodhisattva in Mahāyāna Buddhism practices in order to attain Buddhahood: (1) The perfection of giving (dāna), of three kinds—giving of wealth, of Dharma, and of fearlessness—able to counteract stinginess and greed and to remove poverty. (2) The perfection of moral discipline (śīla): upholding the precepts and constantly self-examining, able to counteract evil karma and make body and mind cool and clear. (3) The perfection of patience (kṣānti): enduring persecution, able to counteract anger and settle the mind. (4) The perfection of vigor (vīrya): advancing without slackening or yielding when practicing the other five virtues, able to counteract indolence and grow wholesome dharmas. (5) The perfection of meditative concentration (dhyāna): cultivating meditation, able to counteract a scattered mind and settle it. (6) The perfection of wisdom (prajñā): able to counteract delusion and open true wisdom, thereby grasping the true meaning of life. These six perfections begin with giving and culminate in wisdom, from which one may know the magnanimous breadth of the Mahāyāna bodhisattva.
