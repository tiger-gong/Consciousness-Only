# birth-and-death
**梵 / Sanskrit**：*saṃsāra*

## Explanation（English）
Means that, on the basis of karmic causes, beings undergo an unending succession of births and deaths within the six deluded destinies—gods, humans, asuras, hungry ghosts, animals, and hells. It is set in contrast to nirvāṇa. Because birth-and-death is endless, it is likened to an ocean and called the “ocean of birth-and-death”; because it is a world of suffering, it is also called the “bitter sea of birth-and-death.” To cross this bitter sea and reach the far shore of nirvāṇa is extremely difficult, hence it is also called the “ocean hard to cross.” Fascicle 8 of the Cheng weishi lun sets out two kinds of birth-and-death: (1) Segmented birth-and-death: with contaminated good and evil karma as cause and the hindrance of afflictions as condition, one receives a coarse retribution within the three realms; the lifespan and bodily size of this retribution are fixed within limits, hence it is called the “segmented body,” and to transmigrate receiving such a body is segmented birth-and-death. (2) Transformational birth-and-death (also “inconceivable transformational birth-and-death”): arhats, pratyekabuddhas, and bodhisattvas of great power, though no longer subject to segmented birth-and-death, take uncontaminated discriminating karma as cause and the hindrance to the knowable as condition, and receive a superior, subtle retribution-body beyond the three realms—the “mind-made body”—with which they re-enter the three realms to cultivate the bodhisattva practice up to Buddhahood. Because of the power of their compassionate vows, the lifespan and body of this received form can be freely transformed and altered without fixed limit, hence it is called the “transformational body,” and receiving such a body is transformational birth-and-death. In addition, the Xianshi lun speaks of four kinds of birth-and-death and the Mohe zhiguan of seven kinds, here omitted.
