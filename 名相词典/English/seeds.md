# seeds
**梵 / Sanskrit**：*bīja*

## Explanation（English）
“Seeds” is a doctrine established by the Dharma-characteristics school and an extremely important basic theory in Consciousness-Only studies. “Seeds” refers to the capacity, within the ālaya-consciousness, to give rise to all contaminated and uncontaminated dharmas. Fascicle 2 of the Cheng weishi lun says: “What dharma here is called a seed? The distinct capacities within the root consciousness (the ālaya) that intimately produce their own fruits. These are neither identical with nor different from the root consciousness and the fruits produced—essence and function, cause and effect, must in principle be so.” Consciousness is a kind of capacity; when this capacity has not yet become active and is in a latent state, it is called not consciousness but seed; when it becomes active, giving rise to present activity, it is called not seed but consciousness. “Present activity” means the function that gives rise to the various distinct phenomena of form and mind. The various form-and-mind phenomena each have their own intimate cause; this intimate cause is called the capacity, also named the seed. Now, all worldly dharmas—the various mental and material phenomena of the world—arise through the manifestation of seeds within the ālaya-consciousness. The ālaya grasps and maintains the seeds of all dharmas and has the power to give rise to the dharmas of form and mind; this power is called the seed. When the latent seeds (the hidden capacities) give rise to the dharmas of form and mind, this is called present activity. Thus seeds, the ālaya-consciousness, and the present-active resultant dharmas it gives rise to—these three have the relation of essence-and-function and cause-and-effect, and so are “neither identical nor different.” Because the root consciousness is essence and the seeds are function, and essence is essence while function is function, they are not identical; but essence is the essence of this function and function is the function of this essence—essence not apart from function, function not apart from essence—so they are not different. Again, between seed and present activity, the seed is cause and present activity is fruit, and cause is cause while fruit is fruit, so they are not identical; but the cause is the cause of this fruit and the fruit is the fruit of this cause, so they are not different. This principle of essence-function and cause-effect “must in principle be so.” See fascicle 7 of the Cheng weishi lun shuji.
