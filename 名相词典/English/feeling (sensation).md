# feeling (sensation)
**梵 / Sanskrit**：*vedanā*

## Explanation（English）
One of the universally active mental factors among the mental factors. Feeling is reception and sensing. Cheng weishi lun says: “Feeling has the nature of receiving the marks of agreeable and disagreeable objects, and the function of giving rise to craving, for it can give rise to the desires to unite, to separate, and to neither.” That is: the self-nature of feeling is reception. “Agreeable, disagreeable, and neither” means: engaging a desirable object is beneficial (agreeable); engaging an undesirable object is harmful (disagreeable); what is neither desirable nor undesirable is “neither”—a neutral object. “Giving rise to craving as function”: when mind-consciousness engages agreeable or disagreeable objects, pleasure or pain arises; toward a pleasant feeling, before obtaining it one craves to obtain it, and having obtained it one craves not to lose it; toward a painful feeling, before obtaining it one wishes fortunately not to obtain it, and having obtained it one wishes to be free of it at once. Such craving constantly arises in dependence on pleasant and painful feeling—hence “giving rise to craving as function.” See Cheng weishi lun, fascicle 6.
