# attention (mental engagement)
**梵 / Sanskrit**：*manaskāra*

## Explanation（English）
One of the mental factors among the five categories of hundred dharmas. It is the sudden alerting that casts the mind upon something, thereby giving rise to mental activity. Attention is what ordinary speech calls “paying attention.” Cheng weishi lun says: “Attention has the nature of being able to alert the mind, and the function of drawing the mind toward the object; that is, this alerting should arouse the mind-seed and draw it to approach the object—hence named attention.” Its function is thus to make the mind arise in alertness and engage its object: this is the beginning of the arising of thought and intention. Alerting the mind has two senses: first, causing a mind that has not yet arisen to arise; second, when the mind has already arisen, drawing it toward the object. See the entry “universally active mental factors.” See Cheng weishi lun, fascicle 6.
