# non-Buddhist paths
**梵 / Sanskrit**：*tīrthika*

## Explanation（English）
Refers to all religions other than Buddhism. In ancient India, Buddhism regarded “outer-path” practitioners as holy recluses worthy of respect, the term meaning “those who teach correctly” or “ascetics”; correspondingly Buddhism called itself the “inner path,” its scriptures the “inner canon,” and non-Buddhist scriptures the “outer canon.” In later ages the term gradually took on the sense of deviant views and false doctrines, and “outer-path” became a contemptuous, dismissive epithet meaning one whose false teaching lies outside the truth. The kinds of outer paths are very many—e.g., the six heterodox masters, the six ascetic outer paths, the sixteen outer paths, the twenty outer paths, the sixty-two views, the ninety-six kinds of outer paths, and so forth.
