# conditioned dharmas
**梵 / Sanskrit**：*saṃskṛta*

## Explanation（English）
“Active/made” (wei) means having something done or fabricated. Abbreviated “conditioned.” It broadly refers to phenomena produced by the coming-together of causes and conditions; narrowly, it points specifically to human fabricating activity. That is, all phenomena that stand in mutual relation and in the flux of arising-and-ceasing, characterized by the four conditioned marks of arising, abiding, changing, and ceasing. In contrast, that which is forever unchanging and absolutely existent is called the unconditioned. According to fascicle 5 of the Jushe lun guangji, the fabricating of causes and conditions is called “activity”; form, mind, and other dharmas arise from causes and conditions and have such causal fabrication, hence are called “conditioned”—so “conditioned” is also another name for dependently arisen dharmas. The Dasheng yizhang says: “‘Active’ has the sense of aggregating, arising, and fabricating; because dharmas have such activity, they are called conditioned.”
