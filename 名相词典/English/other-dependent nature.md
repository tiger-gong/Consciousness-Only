# other-dependent nature
**梵 / Sanskrit**：*paratantra-svabhāva*

## Explanation（English）
Other-dependent nature. Also called the other-dependent characteristic or conditioned self-nature; one of the three self-natures of Consciousness-Only. “Other” means causes and conditions; a dharma that arises depending on causes and conditions is called other-dependent. It belongs to conditioned dharmas; among the hundred dharmas, all but the six unconditioned—ninety-four in all—are included here. The manifold conditions are the four conditions; mind dharmas require all four to arise, while form dharmas need only the causal condition and the dominant condition. All conditioned phenomena arise by the combination of causes and conditions and cease when they disperse—hence existent yet not existent, nonexistent yet not nonexistent: provisionally existent like an illusion. It may further be divided into defiled other-dependence (contaminated and mixed) and pure other-dependence (uncontaminated and pure); pure other-dependence may also be included under the perfectly accomplished nature. See Cheng weishi lun, fascicle 8.
