# conceptualization
> 校准自「perception (conception)」，依 Cook, *Three Texts on Consciousness Only*。

**梵 / Sanskrit**：*saṃjñā*

## Explanation（English）
One of the universal mental activities among the mental activities. Perception is the conceptual function: upon the object engaged, taking its marks of long or short, square or round, and so on. Cheng weishi lun says: “Perception has the nature of taking marks upon the object, and the function of establishing manifold names and words; that is, only when the demarcating marks of the object are set up can manifold names and words arise accordingly.” Perception “takes marks upon the object as nature, and establishes manifold names and words as function.” For example, when visual consciousness cognizes red, it reckons “this is red, not yellow, not blue,” making this demarcation and taking the shared mark; from this taking of an image, names and words are established. The self-nature of perception is only taking an image; yet taking an image is itself establishing names and words—saying “this is red, this is yellow; this is square, this is round”—an unspoken naming. See Cheng weishi lun, fascicle 6.
