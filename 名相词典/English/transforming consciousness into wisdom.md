# transforming consciousness into wisdom
**梵 / Sanskrit**：*āśraya-parāvṛtti*

## Explanation（English）
The Yogācāra school and the Consciousness-Only school hold that, through specific cultivation, upon realizing Buddhahood one can transform the contaminated eight consciousnesses into the uncontaminated, thereby attaining four wisdoms: (1) Transforming the first five consciousnesses (eye, ear, nose, tongue, body) yields the Wisdom of Accomplishing What Is to Be Done; this wisdom, wishing to benefit and gladden all beings, is able to perform good throughout the ten directions for their sake by means of the three karmas of body, speech, and mind. (2) Transforming the sixth consciousness (mental consciousness) yields the Wisdom of Wondrous Observation; this wisdom well observes the particular and shared characteristics of all dharmas, turning without obstruction, able to expound the Dharma freely according to beings' differing capacities and to teach and transform them. (3) Transforming the seventh consciousness (manas) yields the Wisdom of Equality; this wisdom observes that all dharmas, self and other, sentient beings all, are utterly equal, and, ever corresponding with great compassion and the rest, is able to deliver all beings equally and universally. (4) When the eighth consciousness (ālaya) is transformed to the uncontaminated, one attains the Great Perfect Mirror Wisdom; this wisdom is free of all discrimination, its cognitive objects and modes subtle and hard to know, neither deluded nor ignorant—all objective appearances, in nature and characteristic pure, free of all defilement, like the radiance of a great round mirror that reflects the myriad images everywhere without omitting the finest detail. See fascicle 10 of the Cheng weishi lun.
