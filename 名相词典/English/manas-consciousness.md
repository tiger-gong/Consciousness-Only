# manas-consciousness
**梵 / Sanskrit**：*manas*

## Explanation（English）
Sanskrit manas, rendered by meaning as “mind,” with the sense of deliberation. It is the seventh of the “eight ruling minds” established by the Consciousness-Only school. The Mahāyāna Broad Treatise on the Five Aggregates states: “The most excellent mind is the consciousness that takes the storehouse-consciousness as its object; it is constantly concomitant with self-delusion, self-view, self-conceit, and self-love, and continues of one kind from before to after. Excepted are the stages when the arhat’s noble path or the absorption of cessation is presently arisen.” The manas-consciousness constantly clings to the perceiving portion of the eighth, ālaya (storehouse-consciousness), as a “self,” and “constantly and carefully deliberates” upon it. Manas is the faculty on which the sixth, mental consciousness, depends; it is constantly concomitant with the four afflictions of self-delusion, self-view, self-conceit, and self-love, and is of obscuring-neutral nature. It is also the root of self-grasping: if one is deludedly attached, one creates evil karma; conversely, if afflictions and evil karma are cut off and one thoroughly awakens to the truth of the emptiness of persons and dharmas, it is therefore called the defiled-and-pure consciousness, also the deliberating consciousness or the deliberating transformer. See Cheng weishi lun, fascicle 4.
