# emptiness
**梵 / Sanskrit**：*śūnyatā*

## Explanation（English）
An alternative name for suchness (tathatā). The Sanskrit śūnyatā is rendered “emptiness-nature.” Suchness is the true substance free from the two graspings—at a self and at dharmas; therefore, at the point where one cultivates the contemplation of emptiness and departs from these two graspings, the true substance of suchness vividly manifests. This true nature revealed on the basis of emptiness is called “emptiness-nature” (śūnyatā); it does not mean that the substance of suchness is [merely] empty. The Cheng weishi lun shuji, fascicle 1a, says: “The Sanskrit śūnya may be described as being like space; it is termed śūnyatā. Such emptiness-nature is precisely the true nature revealed by the two [kinds of] emptiness. Thus ‘emptiness’ is spoken of from the side of that which reveals; the nature [revealed by] the two emptinesses is named by way of a dependent (tatpuruṣa) compound. To say merely ‘suchness is empty’ would not fully accord with the principle.”
