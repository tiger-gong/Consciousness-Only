# Consciousness-Only school
**梵 / Sanskrit**：*Yogācāra / Vijñaptimātra*

## Explanation（English）
Another name for the Dharma-characteristics school: because it adjudicates the essence and characteristics of all dharmas, it is called the Dharma-characteristics school; because it clarifies the wondrous principle that the myriad dharmas are consciousness-only, it is called the Consciousness-Only school. One of the eight Mahāyāna schools of China, its lineage was transmitted from the Yogācāra school of India. In India, nine hundred years after the Buddha's parinirvāṇa, the bodhisattva Asaṅga was born and went forth into monastic life within the Mahīśāsaka school; later, reading the Mahāyāna scriptures, he turned from the Lesser to the Great and cultivated the Mahāyāna. It is said that the bodhisattva Maitreya in Tuṣita Heaven descended to the Yumana lecture hall in the country of Ayodhyā in central India and expounded the Five Great Treatises for Asaṅga; receiving this teaching, Asaṅga composed many treatises and propagated the tenets of the Dharma-characteristics school. His younger brother Vasubandhu continued this, composing the Treatise on the Five Aggregates, the Treatise on the Hundred Dharmas, the Thirty Verses on Consciousness-Only, the Twenty Verses on Consciousness-Only, and other treatises, thereby bringing the Consciousness-Only school to completion. In India this is also called the Yogācāra school, standing opposed to the Madhyamaka school of the Mahāyāna “emptiness” tradition. In his late years Vasubandhu composed the Thirty Verses, completing only the verse-text and passing away before writing the prose commentary; over the following century, ten great commentator-masters such as Nanda and Dharmapāla each composed a commentary on the Thirty Verses. Dharmapāla's disciple Śīlabhadra thoroughly mastered the profound meaning of Yogācāra and Consciousness-Only and greatly propagated this school at Nālandā monastery. In the Tang dynasty, the Chinese Tripiṭaka-master Xuanzang, during his study-travels in India, studied under Śīlabhadra for five years; on returning home he extensively translated this school's scriptures and treatises and established the school. His disciple Kuiji continued it, composing many commentaries, whereupon the school's framework was complete. This is a summary of the school's lineage. See the entries “Yogācāra school” and “Consciousness-Only school.”
