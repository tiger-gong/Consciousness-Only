# greed
**梵 / Sanskrit**：*rāga*

## Explanation（English）
A root affliction among the mental factors. Greed is the mental function of insatiable craving for the five sense pleasures, fame, wealth, and the like. Toward what one finds agreeable, a defiled attachment arises, which draws forth the five grasping aggregates and produces suffering. Also called craving, lustful attachment, or clinging. The Cheng Weishi Lun says: 'What is greed? Its nature is defiled attachment to existence and the supports of existence; it obstructs nongreed and has the activity of producing suffering, for by the force of craving the grasping aggregates arise.' 'Existence' means the three realms; the 'supports of existence' are the causes that give rise to them—affliction and karma. Greed takes defiled clinging as its nature: clinging to self and what belongs to self, grasping wealth, sensuality, power, and status without release, obstructing nongreed, generating affliction and karma, falling into the three realms, and receiving painful retribution. In short, greed is the pursuit of and attachment to the worldly five desires and six sense objects. Among the minor concomitant afflictions is stinginess: stinginess withholds what should be given; greed takes what should not be taken.
