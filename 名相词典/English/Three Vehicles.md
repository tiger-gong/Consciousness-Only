# Three Vehicles

## Explanation（English）
“Vehicle” means a conveyance; the Three Vehicles are three kinds of conveyance, a metaphor for the three teachings that carry sentient beings across the stream of birth-and-death to the far shore of nirvāṇa. In accordance with beings' differing capacities, the Buddha taught three: the Śrāvaka Vehicle, the Pratyekabuddha Vehicle, and the Bodhisattva Vehicle. (1) The Śrāvaka Vehicle: awakening to the Way by hearing the Buddha's spoken teaching, hence “voice-hearer”; knowing suffering and cutting off its origin, longing for cessation and cultivating the path, they take these Four Truths as their vehicle. (2) The Pratyekabuddha Vehicle (also “solitary-awakened”): awakening to the true principle by contemplating the twelve links of dependent origination, hence “condition-awakened”; first contemplating ignorance through old-age-and-death, then the cessation of ignorance through the cessation of old-age-and-death, and thereby awakening to what is neither arising nor ceasing, they take these twelve links as their vehicle. (3) The Bodhisattva Vehicle (also the Great Vehicle, the Buddha Vehicle): seeking supreme enlightenment, vowing to deliver all beings, cultivating the six perfections and myriad practices, they take these six perfections as their vehicle. The first two vehicles benefit only oneself and not others, so they are together called the Lesser Vehicle; the Bodhisattva Vehicle fully possesses both self-benefit and benefit for others, so it is the Great Vehicle.
