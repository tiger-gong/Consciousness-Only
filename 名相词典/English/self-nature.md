# self-nature
**梵 / Sanskrit**：*svabhāva*

## Explanation（English）
Refers to the intrinsic nature of a thing's own essence; the Consciousness-Only masters mostly call it the “own-mark.” That is, the truly unchanging, pure and unmixed individual character that each dharma possesses is called its self-nature. In other words, self-nature is the essence of a dharma—that which is originally so, forever so, and cannot be altered by any means. In fact, all dharmas are without self-nature; only because they are without self-nature can they be malleable, and only so can there be arising, ceasing, and transformation. The Madhyamaka school holds that all dharmas are formed by the coming-together of causes and conditions and have no fixed self-nature, hence self-nature is empty. Thus the Madhyamaka theory of “dependent arising, empty of nature” is built upon the foundation that all dharmas are without self-nature.
