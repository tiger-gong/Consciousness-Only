# unconditioned dharmas
**梵 / Sanskrit**：*asaṃskṛta*

## Explanation（English）
Among the Five Categories and Hundred Dharmas, the fifth category is the unconditioned dharmas. The Treatise on the Hundred Dharmas says: “Fifth, the unconditioned dharmas, briefly of six kinds: (1) unconditioned space, (2) unconditioned cessation through discrimination, (3) unconditioned cessation not through discrimination, (4) unconditioned immovability, (5) unconditioned cessation of perception-and-feeling, (6) unconditioned suchness.” The unconditioned dharmas are the true dharma-nature of the preceding four categories, hence called the “true nature of consciousness”—that is, the fifth, “consciousness-only as true nature.” Within the Five Categories, the six divisions of mental factors are the concomitants of consciousness, called “concomitant consciousness-only”; the eleven form dharmas are the objects of consciousness, called “objective consciousness-only”; the twenty-four dharmas not concomitant with mind are the positional states of consciousness, called “positional consciousness-only”; and the unconditioned dharmas are the true nature of consciousness, hence called “true-nature consciousness-only.”
